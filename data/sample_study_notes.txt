Indian Polity — Quick Reference for UPSC

The Constitution of India was adopted on 26 November 1949 and came into force on 26 January 1950.
Dr. B.R. Ambedkar is known as the Father of the Indian Constitution.

Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12–35):
- Right to Equality (Articles 14–18)
- Right to Freedom (Articles 19–22)
- Right against Exploitation (Articles 23–24)
- Right to Freedom of Religion (Articles 25–28)
- Cultural and Educational Rights (Articles 29–30)
- Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32) — heart of the Constitution per Ambedkar

Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV, Articles 36–51) are non-justiciable guidelines for governance.

Parliament consists of the President and two Houses: Rajya Sabha (Council of States) and Lok Sabha (House of the People).

The President is elected indirectly by an electoral college. Term: 5 years.

Union Executive: President, Vice-President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers.

Judiciary: Supreme Court at apex; High Courts in states; subordinate courts below.

Federal features with unitary bias: dual polity, written constitution, division of powers, independent judiciary, but single citizenship and integrated judiciary.

Amendment: Article 368 — special majority in Parliament; some provisions need ratification by half the states.

Emergency provisions (Part XVIII):
- National Emergency (Article 352)
- State Emergency / President's Rule (Article 356)
- Financial Emergency (Article 360)

Panchayati Raj: 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 — three-tier system in rural areas.
Urban local bodies: 74th Amendment Act, 1992.

Important commissions: Finance Commission (Article 280), Election Commission (Article 324), UPSC (Article 315).
